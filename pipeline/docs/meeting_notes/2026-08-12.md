## 6. Variables — proposal-template table

Populated in-meeting. Excerpt of the SO₂ row as the template pattern:

| Variable | Operational definition | Unit | Type | Site-time resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SO₂ concentration | Hourly ambient sulfur dioxide (parameter code 42401, method codes 100 + 92, UV-Fluorescence, FEM); days with < 18 valid hours flagged as incomplete | ppb | continuous | site-specific hourly observation |

**Variables agreed for the model:**

- **Pollutants:** SO₂ (42401), Ozone (44201) — both to be modelled.
- **Meteorological:** wind speed, wind direction, temperature, heat
  index, humidity, atmospheric pressure, precipitation.
- **Wind direction encoding — decomposition discussed.** Jasmine +
  Aidan agreed to represent wind direction *both* as continuous
  degrees *and* as decomposed zonal (u) + meridional (v) components
  (avoids the 0°/360° discontinuity — this is the standard atmospheric
  physics encoding, aligns with what Jasmine's NWS lab uses). Also,
  16 categorical sectors for the pollution-rose visualisation.
- **Refinery-Row bearing (new derived variable):** angular difference
  between observed wind direction and the bearing from Refinery-Row
  centroid to each site.
- **Time features:** hour, season, year.
- **Method code:** documented as a supplementary table (not fed to the
  model as a predictor); enables reader-side interpretation of
  cross-year comparability.
- **AQI band:** exploratory categorical target.

**Variables discussed and excluded:**

- **Wind gusts** — too sparse in the Open Weather feed (many null rows
  per hour). Explicitly documented as a study limitation rather than
  quietly dropped.
- **Boundary layer height + temperature inversion** — require weather
  balloon data every 12 h; realistic reliable data acquisition not
  feasible for the current window. Excluded; noted in limitations.

## 8. Sea-breeze convection + cloud cover discussion

Jasmine's meteorological deep-dive during the variable-filling
session (verbatim-ish):

- Corpus Christi's summer precipitation is dominated by **sea-breeze-
  initiated convection** — cumulus / thunderstorm cells that form
  right along the coast when the sea breeze pushes inland.
- **Frequency of these events has declined** over recent years — this
  is what's driving the current drought. Jasmine is doing a separate
  NWS-collaborative study on exactly this decline.
- **Tropical moisture from the Gulf** and, less commonly, **heat
  plumes off central Mexico mountains** crossing the Rio Grande are
  the other precipitation drivers.
- **Wind shear over Corpus Christi is mostly aloft** (higher altitude
  and over water) — shouldn't meaningfully disrupt surface-level
  pollutant transport predictions.
- **Cloud cover** ≈ frontal-system indicator in winter (associated
  with cold fronts and reduced solar radiation → different
  photochemistry). Kept as a covariate.

This discussion informed both the variables table and the eventual
methods-section framing.

## 9. Sensitivity + missing-data policy

- **Daily completeness threshold currently in the pipeline:** days
  with < 18 valid hours are flagged as incomplete. Aidan noted this
  was an exploratory threshold and may need lowering; team agreed to
  treat threshold selection as a **sensitivity analysis** rather than
  a fixed choice.
- **Imputation transparency requirement (Aidan raised, team endorsed).**
  Any imputed data in the final model must have the **percentage
  imputed reported**, both overall and per pollutant. Not doing so
  was flagged as a methodological weakness in our previous time-series
  paper. Dr. Melaram: *"So many studies I've come across do that.
  Like, 'we imputed' — okay, that's it? That's a huge issue."*
- **Missingness-pattern EDA (Dr. Melaram):** required before deciding
  on imputation strategy — "*Do we really understand the pattern of
  missingness of our variables?*"
